# Research Agenda

This agenda identifies public questions guiding ArcTopia-related research. It is intentionally non-enabling and does not disclose private implementation methods.

## 1. Future Capability Preservation

- Which capabilities allow people, institutions, and systems to adapt when conditions change?
- When can present optimization reduce future options, competence, reversibility, repair capacity, or ability to generate alternatives?
- How should retained, reconstructable, and generative capability be distinguished?
- Under what conditions does capability-focused analysis add information beyond conventional performance, risk, resilience, or sustainability measures?
- Where should the theory fail or add no material value?

## 2. Decision-time verification and human control

- When does an earlier authorization remain valid at the moment consequential action is taken?
- Which capability conditions make human oversight practically meaningful rather than merely formal?
- How do competence, independent evidence, intervention, challenge, fallback, and reconsideration change over time?
- What kinds of material change should trigger revalidation?
- When do formal review rights overstate practical agency?

## 3. Transformation memory, provenance, and recovery

- When are present-state descriptions insufficient for consequential evaluation?
- Which pathway facts are material to later decision-making?
- Can two apparently equivalent present states legitimately support different next actions because their histories differ?
- What must remain traceable for later challenge, recovery, reconstruction, or alternative action?
- How should uncertainty and incomplete historical evidence be represented?

## 4. Legitimacy and contestability through change

- When does legitimacy require continued conditions rather than one-time authorization?
- How can responsibility survive organizational, technical, or institutional transformation?
- When should earlier conclusions be reopened, limited, or treated as no longer actionable?
- How should affected parties retain practical capacity to question consequential decisions?

## 5. Empirical testing

- Can trajectory-sensitive information change evaluation beyond a specified present-state baseline?
- Can capability-focused analysis improve prediction, diagnosis, agreement, or decision quality in bounded settings?
- Which comparators provide a credible test rather than an artificially weak baseline?
- Can evaluation rules be frozen before outcomes are known?
- How should null, negative, contradictory, and domain-specific results revise the theory?

## 6. Human-centered computational systems

- What should computational systems be allowed to represent, verify, recommend, or simulate?
- Which authority decisions must remain explicitly human or institutionally accountable?
- How can recorded history, inference, simulation, recommendation, and action remain distinguishable?
- How can technical feasibility be prevented from being mistaken for normative or empirical validation?

## Public research methods

The public programme may include:

- conceptual and theoretical papers;
- formal propositions and falsification criteria;
- literature synthesis;
- retrospective, prospective, and controlled empirical studies;
- synthetic examples and bounded demonstrations;
- transparent comparator design;
- non-enabling conceptual diagrams;
- cross-domain testing.

Private implementation, confidential cases, proprietary datasets, exact technical mechanisms, and patent-sensitive combinations remain outside the public agenda unless separately cleared.
