# Public Concepts

This document describes selected ArcTopia ideas at a deliberately high level.

## 1. Present state is not always enough

Two systems can look equivalent now while differing in the histories, obligations, capabilities, evidence, or authority conditions that produced that state.

For some consequential decisions, those differences may matter.

## 2. Transformation changes future capability

A transformation can improve present performance while reducing what remains practically possible later.

Relevant capabilities may include the ability to act, learn, contest, intervene, coordinate, refuse, repair, reconstruct, adapt, or generate alternatives.

## 3. Earlier approval may not equal decision-time control

An authorization can remain formally recorded even after the competence, evidence, intervention routes, fallback options, or practical agency required to exercise meaningful control have changed.

This motivates research into decision-time validity and revalidation.

## 4. Evidence and authority are different

Evidence can support a conclusion without itself determining who is authorized to decide.

ArcTopia treats evidential support, authorization, and execution conditions as distinct questions.

## 5. Responsibility and contestability should survive change

Transformation should not make responsibility disappear merely because organizations, assets, representations, or technical systems have changed.

Likewise, a decision that is formally reviewable may still become practically difficult to challenge.

## 6. Explanation includes conditions for revision

A useful explanation should not only state why a conclusion was reached. It should also make clear what assumptions, evidence, or changed conditions could require reconsideration.

## 7. Simulation must remain distinguishable from recorded history

Counterfactual analysis can support reasoning, but hypothetical states should not silently overwrite the historical record being evaluated.

## 8. Human authority remains accountable

Computational systems may organize evidence, expose conflicts, test consistency, or model alternatives. These functions do not by themselves confer legal, institutional, or normative authority.

## What this document is not

This is not:

- an algorithm description;
- a patent specification;
- an API contract;
- an implementation guide;
- a scoring model;
- a complete governance protocol;
- evidence of completed empirical validation.

Protected technical work remains outside this public description.
